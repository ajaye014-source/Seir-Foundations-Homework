# Homework Notes — Lesson 2: Slack Alerting

## Experiments
- Experiment 1 Prediction vs. Actual:
  - Prediction: 1 out of 4 alerts will arrive.
  - Actual: Exactly 1 alert arrived (CRITICAL). Setting SLACK_ALERT_SEVERITY=CRITICAL filters out lower severities (INFO, WARNING, ERROR).
- Experiment 2 (Kill Switch):
  - Setting SLACK_ALERTS_ENABLED=false blocked all messages from sending. A kill switch is vital during planned maintenance windows so engineers don't trigger false alarms while taking systems offline.
- Experiment 3 (Threshold Modification):
  - Lowering the WARNING threshold from 75% to 20% caused hw4_disk_watch.py to evaluate my current disk usage (25.7%) as a WARNING instead of INFO.

## Script Questions
- hw2_severity_tour.py:
  - Which severities arrived, and why those?
  - All four severities (INFO, WARNING, ERROR, CRITICAL) arrived because SLACK_ALERT_SEVERITY=INFO allows INFO and all higher-level alerts to pass through.

- hw3_server_time.py:
  - How many hours apart are server time and your wall clock?
  - The server runs on UTC (04:25 UTC). My local wall clock (EST) is 5 hours behind server time.

- hw4_disk_watch.py:
  - Why is a quiet INFO here good design, not a failure?
  - Routine INFO logs confirm the system check ran successfully without causing "alert fatigue" or spamming engineers with unnecessary notifications during normal operations.

- hw5_load_check.py:
  - What would make this number spike on the Balerica bank?
  - A massive spike in customer transactions (e.g., payday morning traffic), intensive database background jobs, or a malicious DDoS attack targeting CPU resources.

- hw6_incident_drill.py:
  - Which fake incident deserves to wake a human at 3 AM?
  - A CRITICAL severity incident, such as a complete database failure or corrupted ledger unreachable error, because it halts core bank operations and requires immediate manual intervention.
